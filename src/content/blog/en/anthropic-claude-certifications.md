Claude certifications are Anthropic's official professional credentials for Claude. There are four. The first, Claude Certified Architect - Foundations, launched in March 2026, and three more followed in July. All four are proctored by Pearson VUE, cost $99 to $175, earn a Credly digital badge, and stay valid for 12 months. Each has an official Exam Guide that lists the exam's domains and what share of the questions each domain carries. None of them is the same thing as the free Claude course certificates that circulate on social media. This article covers which one to take, what each one tests, and how they differ from the free certificates.

## Model vendors have started issuing their own credentials

Until recently, the best-known proctored AI certifications came from the cloud vendors: Microsoft's Azure AI certifications, Google Cloud's [Generative AI Leader](https://cloud.google.com/learn/certification/generative-ai-leader), [AWS Certified AI Practitioner](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/certified-ai-practitioner/). They test how to use AI services on that vendor's platform, and they cost roughly $99 to $200.

By 2026 a second kind had taken shape: the companies that build the models issuing credentials themselves. The three main model vendors are at very different stages:

Two differences are worth spelling out. What most people can get from OpenAI today [proves you finished a course](https://openai.com/index/openai-certificate-courses/), not that you passed an exam. Google does run [proctored exams](https://cloud.google.com/learn/certification/generative-ai-leader), but its cloud division issues them, and they test using AI on Google Cloud, not the Gemini model ecosystem itself.

So far, only one model vendor runs the whole system under its own model brand, with its own exam questions, third-party proctoring and a formal way to register: Anthropic, the enterprise-focused company behind Claude.

In March 2026, Anthropic launched its first credential, Claude Certified Architect - Foundations, together with the Claude Partner Network. The [announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-partner-network) included a line few people noticed: *"Later this year, we'll introduce additional certifications for sellers, architects, and developers."* Four months later, part of that "later" arrived. One credential became four.

The hardest pair to tell apart is CCDV-F and CCAR-F, since both test agents and tools. CCDV-F tests the skill of writing Claude into code: its guide recommends Python or TypeScript, and a third of the questions fall under Applications and Integration, which also covers software engineering basics such as REST APIs and version control. CCAR-F tests architectural judgment and trade-offs. Its guide states that it does not test implementation details of any specific programming language or framework, apart from what configuring tools and schemas requires. Its heaviest domain is agent architecture, and [Claude Code](/en/blog/claude-code-tutorial/) configuration has a domain of its own at 20%.

Once you've picked one, note that registration goes through an Anthropic partner organization; a personal account can't register. If your company isn't a partner, see the last section.

## Free Claude certificates vs. the four paid credentials

Before going further, one mix-up you have probably already run into.

The "free Claude certificates" shared widely on social media are completion certificates from Anthropic's official [course catalog](https://anthropic.skilljar.com/). It has more than twenty free courses, from Claude Code in Action, Introduction to Model Context Protocol and Building with the Claude API to the AI Fluency series for non-technical roles. Finish one and you get a course-completion certificate.

Those certificates are a different thing from the four Claude certifications in this article (CCAO-F, CCDV-F, CCAR-F, CCAR-P).

A course certificate records that you finished an online course. It has no proctoring, no common passing standard and no Credly badge. The four credentials are standardized exams proctored by Pearson VUE, scored on one global scale.

Social media often treats the two as the same. When you see a headline about an "Anthropic certificate", check which kind it means.

## Are CCAF, CCA-F and CCAR-F the same Claude Certified Architect credential?

If you have searched for the Architect - Foundations credential before, you have probably met three different abbreviations. They aren't typos. Two date from the credential's first months, one used by Anthropic and the other by most community posts and third-party write-ups; the third is the formal exam code added in July.

When Anthropic launched the credential in March 2026, the announcement used only the full name, with no formal abbreviation. Anthropic's own follow-up communications, including an official email and the short URL [clau.de/CCAF](https://clau.de/CCAF), used **CCAF**. Community posts and most third-party write-ups used **CCA-F**. You may also see it called "Anthropic Certified Architect"; it's the same exam.

In July 2026, when the program expanded to four credentials and the exams moved to Pearson VUE, Architect - Foundations got its formal exam code: **CCAR-F**. The added R appears to be there to distinguish it from the Associate credential launched at the same time, CCAO-F.

All three abbreviations name the same credential at different times. For anything external-facing, such as a LinkedIn certification entry or a résumé, the current official code CCAR-F is the most precise.

## Why four credentials at once

The four credentials are cut by role: one for people who don't code, one for developers, and two for architects (foundations and professional). When Anthropic released them in July, its [announcement](https://claude.com/blog/four-role-based-claude-certifications) called them "role-based certifications."

The one most worth noticing is CCAO-F (Associate), for non-technical roles. A PM who evaluates output quality, an operations lead who improves processes, an HR manager who plans internal training: Anthropic set an exam for them too, which counts these people among those an [AI adoption](/en/blog/enterprise-ai-adoption-bottleneck/) has to train.

The division of labor is written into the official documents. The [CCAO-F Exam Guide](https://everpath-course-content.s3-accelerate.amazonaws.com/instructor%2F6nizmqk8tpzpfjvt6qmmav7rh%2Fpublic%2F1783542847%2FClaude+Certified+Associate+%E2%80%93+Foundations+Exam+Guide.pdf) lists knowing your limits as an Associate skill: more complex or technical work goes to a Claude Architect or Developer. The guide also states that designing enterprise architecture and building system integrations are outside this credential's scope; they belong to the other two kinds.

In its March [announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-partner-network), Anthropic said certifications for sellers would come later in the year. As of mid-November, the official list still has these four.

The division of labor in AI services work is starting to exist on paper.

Every exam is proctored by Pearson VUE, online or at a test center. The passing score is 720 on a scaled range of 100 to 1,000, and passing earns a Credly digital badge.

**After you pass: renewal.** A credential is valid for 12 months. Before it expires, you can renew for free with a non-proctored online assessment on Partner Academy, without another Pearson VUE exam. If it lapses, you pay the full fee and retake the whole exam. All four guides state one exception: when exam content changes substantially, Anthropic may require holders to retake the full exam instead of the renewal assessment.

**If you fail: waiting periods.** After a first failed attempt you wait 14 days, after a second 30 days, and after a third 90 days. You can sit the same exam at most four times within 12 months, and each attempt costs the full fee again. The limits count per exam, so failing one doesn't stop you from registering for another.

A few more rules decide whether you get into the exam and whether you get your fee back. On exam day you need a valid, government-issued photo ID; if its name doesn't match your registration, you can't test that day and the fee isn't refunded. To change the name on your registration, use Update Exam Profile in your Partner Academy profile. You can do this once a year, and your name locks for a year once you confirm. If you've already used that yearly change and still need a correction, request it from Anthropic Certification Support. Missing a scheduled exam also forfeits the fee.

## How to read the official Exam Guides

If you're preparing seriously, read the four official Exam Guides before any third-party prep material. The PDFs download without a login. Direct links are in the table above, and the [Partner Academy certifications page](https://anthropic-partners.skilljar.com/page/partner-certifications) lists them too.

The four guides share a structure. Every exam is 120 minutes of multiple-choice questions, mixing single-answer and multiple-answer items, and each question states how many answers to pick. Start with three parts:

- Domain weights: what share of the questions each domain carries.
- Sample questions: answers and explanations included, so you can see how questions are written and how hard they are.
- The score report description: besides pass or fail and your score, the report lists your percentage correct in each domain, so a candidate who fails can see what to work on.

Which version to read depends on your exam date:

<div class="k">The same three on or after December 7, 2026: read the new guides, posted from November 17.</div>
<div class="k">CCAR-F isn't part of this update; keep reading the current v1.0.</div>

**Six scenarios** set how it is tested. Questions don't ask for definitions; they put you inside a real project: customer support agent, code generation, multi-agent research, developer productivity, CI/CD and structured data extraction. Each exam draws 4 of the 6 at random.

Here is how the two layers fit together. Say you draw the customer support agent scenario. The setup is a support system built on Claude, and the agent has four tools: look up a customer, look up an order, issue a refund, and escalate to a human. The question asks whether an action that must never go wrong, like a refund, should be blocked in code or left to a reminder in the prompt. The question is dressed as customer support, but it tests the Agentic Architecture & Orchestration domain, specifically enforcing a workflow in code.

The guide also lists what the exam leaves out: fine-tuning or training models, implementation details of embedding models and vector databases, computer use (operating a browser or desktop), and implementing the streaming API. You can set those aside while preparing.

The CCAR-F guide is also much thicker, at 39 pages, with 4 hands-on exercises and 12 sample questions; the other three guides have 3 sample questions each. I took CCAR-F myself and scored 882; my prep method, domain map and test-day walkthrough are in [my CCAR-F exam experience](/en/blog/ccar-f-exam-experience/).

## My company isn't an Anthropic partner. Can I still take it?

**Yes, but you register through a partner organization.** All four credentials are registered on Partner Academy and paid for when you schedule at Pearson VUE. Anthropic opens registration only to members of the Claude Partner Network, its partner program for service providers, and a personal email can't register. If your company isn't a partner, in practice you need an account through a partner organization. If that's where you're stuck, the later part of [my CCAR-F exam experience](/en/blog/ccar-f-exam-experience/) explains how I can help.

**Whether it's worth taking depends on whether your clients, or the company you want to work for, use Claude.** A credential's weight ultimately comes from the real products and the real market behind it. That is why the model-vendor category is worth watching: it has only just opened.
